`investor_flow` answers one question: **what did each class of investor do on a given
session?** Foreign investors, institutionals, individuals and the rest, bought and sold, in
R$ thousands.

| Endpoint | Grain | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /investor_flow` | `(investor_type, reference_date)` | Daily buy / sell / net flow, plus the month-to-date figures it was derived from |

It is a **view**: read it with `GET`, filter with PostgREST syntax
(`?investor_type=eq....`), page with `limit`/`offset`. The securities-lending views are on
their own page, [Securities lending](/api-docs/lending).

<Warning>
  **These numbers are a first difference that SILO computes. B3 does not publish a daily
  flow series.**

  What B3 publishes is a **month-to-date cumulative snapshot** — "so far this month,
  investor type X has bought R$ N" — released with a **T+2 lag**. A day's flow is therefore
  the difference between two consecutive snapshots, and that arithmetic has two ways of
  going badly wrong. Both are handled here rather than left to you:

  - **The difference never reaches across a month boundary.** Subtracting January's last
    snapshot from February's first would report a whole month of buying as one day's flow.
    The window is partitioned by month, so it simply cannot.
  - **The first snapshot held for a month is not always that month's first session.** When
    the pipeline was first deployed it was mid-month. Treating that month-to-date total as
    one day's flow would invent a spike on an arbitrary date. So it counts as a daily flow
    **only** when it really is the month's first session; otherwise the flow is `null` and
    `flow_basis` says why.

  Combined with the ratchet below, this means the flow series has genuine holes near the
  start of any month SILO began capturing mid-way through. They are marked, not filled.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **History starts at first capture and cannot be extended backwards.**

  `b3_investor_participation` is part of B3's BDI group, which B3 retains for roughly **21
  business days** and publishes no archive for. A missed session is lost at any price —
  there is no backfill to run. Do not read the first date in this view as a market event,
  and do not compute a long-run average or a year-over-year comparison until the warehouse
  has actually held that much. [`coverage`](/api-docs/coverage) reports the real span on
  the `investor_flow` row and repeats this in `notes`.

  The T+2 publication lag also means `as_of` trails the calendar by two sessions **even
  when ingest is perfectly healthy**. That is the source's cadence, not staleness on our
  side — `landed_at` on the same coverage row is the one that answers "did the pipeline
  run?".
</Warning>

## `GET /investor_flow`

### Parameters

#### `investor_type`

`eq.<type>` — the investor class exactly as B3 publishes it. The distinct values are
small in number and come straight from the source; read them off the data rather than
hard-coding a translation.


#### `reference_date`

`eq.2026-09-16`, or `gte.` / `lte.` for a window. This is the session the flow is **for**,
not the date B3 published it — the T+2 lag is already accounted for.


#### `flow_basis`

`eq.delta`, `eq.month_open` or `eq.unknown_opening_snapshot`. Filtering to
`in.(delta,month_open)` is the usual way to drop the uninterpretable rows — see below.


### Read `flow_basis` before you read anything else

<Warning>
  **Rows the view cannot interpret carry `null` flows — never zeros — and summing them as
  zeros silently understates the month.**

  | `flow_basis` | Means | `*_value_thousands` |
  |---|---|---|
  | `delta` | A real one-session difference between two consecutive snapshots | A flow |
  | `month_open` | The month's first session, where the month-to-date total *is* the day | A flow |
  | `unknown_opening_snapshot` | No earlier snapshot is held for that month, so the day's flow is unknowable | **`null`, by construction** |

  `null` here is a statement: *we do not know what happened on this day*. It is not zero
  flow, not a quiet session, and not missing data to be filled. A `sum()` in SQL or pandas
  will skip nulls silently and hand you a month total that is missing an unknown number of
  days — which looks exactly like a real, smaller number.

  **Filter explicitly.** Either restrict to `flow_basis=in.(delta,month_open)` and know
  your window is partial, or keep every row and treat the nulls as the gaps they are. What
  you must not do is aggregate across the three bases without looking.
</Warning>

### Returns

#### `reference_date`

The session the flow is for. Part of the grain.

#### `investor_type`

Investor class as B3 publishes it. Part of the grain.

#### `flow_basis`

`delta` | `month_open` | `unknown_opening_snapshot`. **Read this first.**

#### `buy_value_thousands`

The session's purchases, in **R$ thousands**. `null` when
`flow_basis = unknown_opening_snapshot`.

#### `sell_value_thousands`

The session's sales, R$ thousands. `null` on the same rows.

#### `net_value_thousands`

`buy - sell` for the session, R$ thousands. Positive is net buying. `null` on the same
rows.

#### `mtd_buy_value_thousands`

B3's **cumulative** month-to-date purchases as published — the figure the difference was
taken from. Never null: this is the source value, kept so you can verify the arithmetic
rather than trust it.

#### `mtd_sell_value_thousands`

Cumulative month-to-date sales as published.


<Note>
  **Units are R$ thousands, from the source's own `brl_mil` fields** (*mil* is Portuguese
  for thousand). Multiply by 1,000 for reais; do not assume millions because a headline
  elsewhere quotes them that way.
</Note>

<Note>
  The `mtd_*` columns are the check on everything above them. Within one month,
  `mtd_buy_value_thousands` on consecutive sessions should differ by exactly
  `buy_value_thousands` on the later one. If you ever need to defend a flow figure, that
  subtraction is the audit — and it is why the cumulative columns are published beside the
  differenced ones instead of being discarded.
</Note>

### Example

One investor type across a month, with the uninterpretable rows kept visible:

<CodeGroup>

```bash curl
curl -s -G "$SILO_URL/investor_flow" \
  -H "apikey: $SILO_ANON_KEY" \
  --data-urlencode "select=reference_date,investor_type,flow_basis,net_value_thousands,mtd_buy_value_thousands" \
  --data-urlencode "investor_type=eq.Investidor Estrangeiro" \
  --data-urlencode "reference_date=gte.2026-09-01" \
  --data-urlencode "order=reference_date.asc"
```

```python Python
import pandas as pd

params = {
    "investor_type": "eq.Investidor Estrangeiro",
    "reference_date": "gte.2026-09-01",
    "order": "reference_date.asc",
}
df = pd.DataFrame(requests.get(f"{URL}/investor_flow", headers=H, params=params).json())

# Look at the bases BEFORE aggregating. A silent sum over all three would drop
# the unknown days and report a smaller month as if it were complete.
print(df["flow_basis"].value_counts())

usable = df[df["flow_basis"].isin(["delta", "month_open"])]
print(usable["net_value_thousands"].sum(),
      "R$ thousands over", len(usable), "of", len(df), "sessions")
```

## What this is not

- **Not a flow by security.** The grain is investor *type*, market-wide. There is no
  per-ticker breakdown in the source, so no join to `quotes` or
  [`short_interest`](/api-docs/lending) exists at this grain. Aligning a market-wide flow
  series against one ticker's return is your inference to make and own, not an edge this
  warehouse publishes.
- **Not a position.** These are gross purchases and sales over a session. A net figure is
  the session's direction, not anyone's holdings.
- **Not a panel metric.** `investor_flow` rows carry no id in the panel's sense, so there is
  no [`panel`](/api-docs/panel) arm for them — the same reason
  [`anbima_classes`](/api-docs/anbima) has none.

## Row caps and paging

The 1,000-row page is PostgREST's `db-max-rows`, and on a view it **cuts silently with a
200**, keeping the oldest rows. Send `Prefer: count=exact` and read `Content-Range`
(`0-999/*` means cut), or page with `limit`/`offset` and an explicit `order`. The
[Python SDK](/api-docs/sdk) does both: `silo.investor_flow(...)` for one page,
`silo.view_all("investor_flow", order="reference_date.asc")` to walk them all.

Given the retention window and a handful of investor types, a whole capture window fits
comfortably inside one page today — but do not build on that; it is a property of the
ratchet, not a guarantee.

## Empty and error behavior

- **Unknown investor type → `200 []`.** Spelling must match B3's; read the distinct values
  off the data rather than guessing a translation.
- **Empty window → `200 []`.** With ~21 business days of retention and a T+2 lag this is
  common. Check [`coverage`](/api-docs/coverage)'s `investor_flow` row first.
- The landing tables (`b3_investor_participation`,
  `b3_investor_participation_monthly`) are **not** readable by client roles; this view is
  owner-privileged and the columns it selects are the whole boundary.
