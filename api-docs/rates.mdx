### Overview

Four functions over two B3 files Silo ingests daily. Nothing is interpolated,
rolled or spliced; the one derived column is `contract_month`.

- **`future_curve(p_root, p_trade_date)`** — every outright contract of one root
  (`DI1`, `DOL` or `WDO`) on one session, nearest maturity first. Default root
  `DI1`; default session: the newest one held for that root.
- **`future_series(p_ticker, p_from, p_to)`** — one DI1, DOL or WDO contract
  through time.
  The same contracts are in [`panel`](/api-docs/panel) as `id_type` `future`
  (`settlement_rate`, `settlement_price`, `open_interest`), next to tickers and funds.
- **`curve(p_curve, p_trade_date)`** — one reference curve on one session, every
  vertex. Default: `PRE`, the newest session held.
- **`curve_history(p_curve, p_tenor_days, p_from, p_to)`** — one of B3's fixed
  vertices through time.

```python
silo.future_curve()                              # the newest session
silo.future_series("DI1F27", start="2026-01-01")
silo.curve("DPL", "2026-09-25")                  # the IPCA coupon
silo.curve_history("PRE", 360)                   # B3's 360-day vertex, 12 months
```

### Calling them

The same four functions answer on every surface. Pick the root or the curve
with one argument; leave the date out for the newest session.

| You want | Call |
| --- | --- |
| The DI1 curve on a session | `future_curve` with `p_root` `DI1` (the default) |
| The dollar futures on a session | `future_curve` with `p_root` `DOL` or `WDO` |
| One contract through time | `future_series` with `p_ticker` such as `DI1F27`, `DOLF27`, `WDOF27` |
| B3's DI x pré, dollar coupon or IPCA coupon curve | `curve` with `p_curve` `PRE`, `DOC` or `DPL` |
| One fixed vertex through time | `curve_history` with `p_curve` and `p_tenor_days` (30, 90, 360, 720 …) |

<CodeGroup>

curl -s -X POST "$SILO_URL/rpc/future_curve" \

  -d '{"p_curve": "DOC", "p_tenor_days": 360, "p_from": "2025-01-01"}'

The SDK methods take friendlier names (`root`, `ticker`, `start`, `end`);
`silo.rpc(name, **params)` takes the SQL names and checks them before sending.
The local `serve/` adapter has no `/v1` route for these four: use the Data API,
the SDK or the [MCP server](/api-docs/mcp).

### DI1 futures

The source is B3's Price Report (`BVBG.086.01`), one row per session and
outright DI1 contract, from **2018-01-02**; B3's older reports are empty.

- **DI1 is quoted in rate.** `settlement_rate` and the `open_px` / `low_px` /
  `high_px` / `avg_px` / `close_px` columns are % a.a. on 252 business days, so
  the **low rate is the high price**. `settlement_price` is the PU: R$ per
  contract, 100,000 at maturity.
- **`contract_month`** is the first day of the month the ticker names, read with
  B3's month letters (`F` January, `G` February, `H` March, `J` April, `K` May,
  `M` June, `N` July, `Q` August, `U` September, `V` October, `X` November, `Z`
  December). The contract matures on that month's first business day. The
  business days to maturity are not stored and not computed here.
- **Nothing is made continuous.** `future_series` follows one contract from
  listing to expiry. For a constant maturity, read B3's own curve below.

### Dollar futures (DOL, WDO)

`future_curve("DOL")` and `future_curve("WDO")` return the same columns as DI1,
but the dollar futures are **quoted in price, not rate**: BRL per USD 1,000, per
B3's contract specification (DOL is USD 50,000 per contract, WDO the mini USD
10,000; both mature on the first business day of the contract month). The
`open_px` / `low_px` / `high_px` / `avg_px` / `close_px` columns and
`settlement_price` are that price exactly as B3 publishes it, with no
conversion, and `settlement_rate` is `null` where B3 prints none. Any other
root is refused with `22023`, which lists the three.

COTAHIST has no futures: the `DOL*` codes in the cash tape (DOLA11, DOLB11 …)
are ETFs, not the future.

### Reference curves

The source is B3's reference-rate file (`TaxaSwap.txt`), every vertex of three
curves, from **2008-01-02**. Read `rate_basis` before comparing two curves.

| `p_curve` | Is | `rate_basis` | B3 fits it to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PRE` | DI x pré, the nominal curve | `exp_252`: % a.a. compounded on 252 business days | DI1 futures (a vertex at every DI1 maturity equals that contract's settlement rate) |
| `DOC` | the clean onshore dollar coupon (cupom cambial limpo) | `linear_360`: % a.a. LINEAR on 360 calendar days, factor `1 + rate × calendar_days / 36000` | DDI futures |
| `DPL` | the clean IPCA coupon, a real rate | `exp_252` | DAP futures, then ANBIMA's NTN-B indicative rates |

B3 defines implied inflation as `(1 + PRE) / (1 + DPL) − 1` at the same tenor.
It carries an inflation risk premium: market pricing, not an expectation.
`DPL`'s shortest vertices lean on the current month's IPCA projection.

<Warning>
  **The long end of every curve is B3's extrapolation, not a price.** Past the last
  maturity of the contract that anchors a curve, B3 extends the last forward rate
  (Manual de Curvas v21). On 2026-09-25, `PRE` ran to 34 years while the last DI1
  contract matured in about 14. Where the tail starts moves as contracts list and
  expire.
</Warning>

### Fixed and moving vertices

B3 publishes every curve on one grid:

- **Fixed vertices** (`vertex_type = F`) sit at nominal tenors, which `vertex_code`
  carries: 1, 7, 14, 21, 30, 60, 90 … 360 … 720 … 1800 … 10800 calendar days.
  The actual `calendar_days` is a day or two longer when the nominal date is not a
  business day. The 30-day vertex was 30 days on 2008-01-02 and 31 on 2026-09-25.
- **Moving vertices** (`M`) sit at the maturities of B3's contracts.

`curve_history` serves a fixed vertex by its nominal tenor (`p_tenor_days`). Any
other tenor raises `22023` listing the fixed ones, because an interpolated tenor
is a choice of method. Read `curve` and interpolate in the notebook; B3's own
method for `PRE` and `DPL` is flat-forward on 252 business days.

### Freshness

[`coverage()`](/api-docs/coverage) has a `di_futures` row (DI1), a
`dollar_futures` row (DOL and WDO) and a `reference_curves` row. The two futures
rows read the same file, so they share a `landed_at`; the reference-rate file
logs its own. Both futures histories miss 2021-01-04 (B3 serves a malformed
file) and 2021-06-10 (an empty one). History comes
from a backfill, so a span that starts later than the dates above was not
loaded; it is not missing at B3.

### Limits

All four refuse above one 1,000-row page (`22023`). One session is about 50 DI1
contracts and about 300 vertices per curve. For the two histories, narrow
`p_from` / `p_to`: a daily series fits about four years per call.
