## 0. TL;DR

1. **I could not find the quote.** Nothing like "we don't even know which agents are running because they self-update" appears in any public transcript I could get. I read two in full: the Sept 16, 2026 Talkenização episode about Liqi's agents, and the April 29, 2026 "Liqi faz 5 anos" episode. The closest things said on the record are (a) agent "identities" get updated as the agents take on more tasks, and (b) conversational agents "autocorrect" to each user's preferences. On auditability, the executives say the **opposite** of the reported quote: "Tudo que eles fazem é registrado e fica auditável, sem limite de tempo." Part 2 of that episode is announced with the hook "quem garante que esses agentes não erram, quem vigia eles?" and was **not yet published** when I checked. Pedro may have heard it there, at a live event or in a private conversation. I cannot confirm it.
2. **For Tomé, Liqi's own public page contradicts "self-updating".** `/como-funciona` says the improvement loop is "automático até a fila". Detecting, logging and prioritizing a gap is automatic. "A construção da capacidade nova ainda passa pela mão de quem desenvolve." The page also says outright that claiming otherwise would be an unsupported claim.
3. **Liqi's public agent counts don't agree with each other.** Depending on source and date they say 13, 15+, 16, 17, 18 or 20+ agents, plus "centenas" or "400+" contract microagents. Tomé's own pipeline figures also differ by source: 71 lines and ~1,900 runs/day on Aug 8, versus 144 pipelines and 201 runs/day on Sept 17. **[inference]** An outsider cannot rebuild an inventory of what is running from public material. That fits the spirit of Pedro's quote, even though the words themselves aren't on record.
4. **Tomé's data-quality engineering is serious and documented.** An LLM proposes, and a deterministic program then checks that the quoted phrase exists on that page (22,447 fabricated citations blocked). A stronger second model audits a sample (12.2% of audited documents had a substantive problem). Every production line "starts the day red". A third state, "eu não olhei", is kept separate from pass and fail. Seven checks run on every answer. The public material says nothing about **which model or prompt version** produced a given fact, or whether an old number can be reproduced.

### 2.2 Tomé's own pipeline (as Liqi describes it)

All figures below come from https://www.agentetome.com/como-funciona, "medido em 08/08/2026", unless another source is given.

- **Scale:** 71 production lines, 70 of them running. 9 public sources, 36 document types, 28 extraction scripts ("roteiros de extração"), "de 1.700 a 2.000 execuções por dia, quase todas de madrugada", 143 query tools in 12 families, 409 distinct fields, 12,683 auto-generated public pages.
- **Code and data:** 450k LOC in 1,421 files; **317 schema migrations "cada uma versionada e reversível"**; 520 engineering documents (150k lines); 339 tables plus 24 precomputed; 1,139 indexes; 222 GB; 130,214 documents "nada apagado"; 2.3M pages; 340k+ facts, "cada um com citação".
- **Later figures (Sept 17, 2026, attributed to Coquieri):** 144 automated pipelines ("esteiras"), **201 executions/day**, 732,642 LOC, 7,903 commits, "O desenvolvimento começou em 16 de julho de 2026". Source: https://bitnoticias.com.br/tecnologia/agente-tome-cvm-2-3-milhoes-documentos-63-dias. The 201 runs/day conflicts with the ~1,900 runs/day on como-funciona. They may count different units; neither source explains. **"88 pipelines" appears in no source I found.** The published figures are 70/71 (Aug 8) and 144 (Sept 17).
- **Pipeline stations (8):** discover → queue with lease/ownership → polite download (≈200k downloads in 11 days, 0.15% failure, zero blocks) → text/OCR (95.4% of image-only docs recovered) → text→fact extraction with page citation → **"Duvidar do que acabou de ler"** (quote must exist, value plausible, date coherent on the fund timeline) → store for comparison, with dedup of republished identical docs (7% of corpus) → available to the chat. Median latency ~14 h, p90 <22 h.
- **The central architectural rule:** "quem lê não pode ser quem aprova. No Tomé, um modelo de IA lê e propõe; depois, um programa comum — sem inteligência artificial nenhuma… vai ao documento e confere se aquela frase existe mesmo, naquela página, e se o número é plausível. O que não passa é descartado, não corrigido."
  - 22,447 fabricated citations blocked, about 1 per 9 approved facts. In regulations, about 1 proposal is rejected for every 2 accepted.
  - **12.2%** "dos documentos auditados por um segundo modelo, mais forte, tinham algum problema de substância". This is labeled "auditoria contínua por amostragem, não censo".
- **Answer path:** "o modelo não tem permissão de gerar número". It can only choose among 143+ tools. Answers then pass **"sete conferências antes de aparecer na sua tela"**. In one recent sample, 371 attempts by the model to go outside the rules were denied by the system. The seven checks are not listed individually on the page.
- **Self-monitoring:** about 1 in 5 automated tasks exists only to check the others. Each of the 70 lines "começa o dia reprovada" and turns green only if it ran **and produced output**. An hourly freshness check runs 36 document categories against their legal deadlines. The "regra dos três nós" requires declared origin, storage and a tool that can read it. An external watchdog in another datacenter puts up a maintenance page in about 4 min. A 1,004-question gold set draws 36 fixed questions plus random ones each run, with independent judges agreeing 81% of the time. One alert gateway: 74 alerts → 15 interruptions. "'Não consegui conferir' nunca é a mesma coisa que 'está tudo certo'", treated as a third state.
- **Versioning of source data:** 28,365 corrected informes stored as before/after pairs across 3,180 funds; 10,332 regulation versions; mean correction lag 312 days. Backups go off-machine 6×/day, and a full restore drill took 25 min.
- **Honest coverage:** 96.2% of active FIDC/FII/FIAGRO funds have a read document. Regulations are near 70%. The page says it always reports the "pior estado, nunca a média".
- **The build queue (1,409 gaps):** "quando a gaveta volta vazia… o Tomé diz que não tem o dado, e registra o pedido para a equipe ir buscar. Já são 1.409 lacunas." The loop runs "Alguém pergunta → Ele admite e anota → Vira fila → A próxima pessoa recebe". Crucially: **"ele é automático até a fila. Detectar que faltou, registrar, medir quantas pessoas pediram a mesma coisa, priorizar — isso acontece sozinho. A construção da capacidade nova ainda passa pela mão de quem desenvolve. O caminho é esse trecho encurtar até fechar sozinho. Não estamos lá."**
- **Coverage growth** has two queues: forward (today's publications) and backward (historical backfill "ano a ano", reaching 2016 for several document types).

## 3. Analysis (all of this section is [inference])

### 3.1 Is Liqi's coverage velocity structural?

**Partly. It is structural in the reading layer but not yet in the build layer.**

- **Structural (the machine does it without a human):** forward discovery and backfill across 36 document types; extraction runs at 1.7–2k/day; freshness checks tied to legal deadlines; gap detection and prioritization. Once an extractor for a document type exists, coverage of that type grows by itself in both directions. That explains the jump from 2.3M pages (Aug 8) to 8.6M pages (Sept 17, bitnoticias).
- **Not (yet) structural:** new _capabilities_, meaning a new document type, field, tool or join, still go through "a mão de quem desenvolve" (Liqi's own words). What makes that fast is AI-assisted engineering: 7,903 commits and about 733k LOC by Sept 17, reportedly from a July 16, 2026 start. That is roughly 125 commits/day, and 317 migrations appeared by Aug 8. Liqi's roster names an agent, Coquito, that "implementa as features escrevendo e revisando código de produção" for its internal platform. Nothing public says Coquito writes Tomé's code, but the rate is only plausible if most of the code is agent-written.
- So the "self-updating" story, if Coquieri told it, fits the **internal fleet** (identities updated, preference learning, correction-driven learning in Fina, Cérebro "autocorrigindo") much better than it fits Tomé. On Tomé's public page Liqi explicitly denies closing the loop on its own.

### 3.2 What it costs in auditability and lineage

Tomé's controls are built around **one question: is this number really in the document?** They answer it very well: deterministic quote verification, a plausibility gate, discard rather than repair. That is a strong _fact-level provenance_ guarantee.

They say little about **process lineage**, meaning which code and model produced the fact and whether the same inputs would produce the same fact again:

- The extraction step is a non-deterministic LLM, and Liqi says so: "Peça a uma IA para ler o mesmo documento duas vezes e você recebe duas respostas diferentes". The quote verifier confirms the phrase exists. It does **not** confirm that the extractor picked the _right_ phrase when several plausible ones exist, such as the wrong tranche, the wrong period, or a superseded clause. The 12.2% second-model finding measures exactly this residue, and it comes from a sample, not a census.
- With ~125 commits/day, 28 extraction scripts, 143 tools and unversioned (publicly) models and prompts, the version of the extractor that wrote a given fact is almost certainly not a stable, citable object. That holds even if git has it internally.
- In the internal fleet, agents whose identities are updated in place, and which learn from corrections in use, are the textbook case where "which version of Fina classified this expense in March?" becomes hard to answer. The "everything is logged" claim covers _actions_, not _agent state at the time of the action_.
- The inconsistent public metrics (13/16/17/18/20+ agents; 71 vs 144 pipelines; 1,900 vs 201 runs/day) suggest that the system's own self-description is produced ad hoc rather than from a registry. That is the observable version of "we don't know which agents are running".

### 3.3 What a regulated buyer could not answer about a Tomé number (from public material)

A compliance officer, auditor or allocator holding a Tomé figure, e.g. "FIDC X subordinação 23.4%, folga 3.4 p.p. vs covenant", could answer: _which document, which page, which quoted phrase, which competência and protocol_. They could **not** answer, from anything published:

1. **Which extractor version, model and prompt produced it**, and whether that version has since been changed or retired.
2. **Reproducibility:** would re-running today give the same number? LLM extraction is admitted to be non-deterministic.
3. **Whether the number sat in the 12.2%-problem population.** Is there a per-fact flag saying second-model audited / not audited / audited-with-finding?
4. **Derived-number lineage:** the "folga" combines a regulation-extracted covenant with an informe series. Which regulation version applied at which competência? The page says regulations are versioned. It does not say the join is versioned or point-in-time.
5. **When the fact entered the store and whether it changed after an answer was given.** There is an as-of or point-in-time guarantee for source restatements, but none is stated for Liqi's _own_ re-extractions.
6. **Coverage denominators per answer:** the fleet reports "pior estado" globally. Does a peer-median answer tell you how many peers had a read document for that month?
7. **Conflict of interest controls:** Liqi structures securitizations and Tomé reads competitors' filings. Beyond the stated policy ("sem distinção de quem os estruturou", /liqi), nothing public describes separation, for example whether Cérebro, which "tá vendo tudo" internally, has any path into Tomé.
8. **Change control:** there is no public changelog, model card, SLA or incident log for Tomé. Enterprise mentions "frescor garantido" but publishes no terms.

For a FIDC allocator writing an investment memo or a compliance file, items 1, 2, 4 and 5 are what an auditor asks first. Tomé is excellent for **finding and citing** the source. It cannot yet be relied on as the **system of record** for the number itself.

### 3.4 What SILO should copy vs avoid

SILO's context: one founder plus Claude Code sessions; GitHub Actions; structured CSV/ZIP sources (not PDFs); 1,463 offline tests; `verify_pipeline.py` as a quality gate; exactly one `cvm_ingest_log` row per ingest; named UNIQUE natural keys; the integrity rules in CLAUDE.md.

**Copy (cheap, high leverage):**

1. **"Every line starts the day red; ran AND produced."** SILO already separates `landed_at` from `complete_through`. A daily check that fails when a scheduled slice _ran successfully but upserted 0 rows where the prior N runs didn't_ would catch the silent-empty failure mode Liqi calls "o pior defeito possível". Most of it already lives in `cvm_ingest_log`.
2. **A third state, "didn't check".** DB Health and `verify_pipeline.py` outputs should distinguish _unknown / check could not run_ from _pass_. CLAUDE.md already warns against crying wolf. This is the complement: never let silence look green.
3. **The gap log as the build queue.** SILO has no chat, but it has an API. Logging `api.*` calls that return empty for a valid key, and 404s on the catalog, gives a measured demand queue. That is Liqi's "1,409 lacunas" without an LLM.
4. **Public "how it works" with measured numbers and stated limits.** `/como-funciona` is a trust asset: dated metrics, worst-case coverage rather than the average, an explicit "what it doesn't do". SILO's `api.coverage()` plus a narrative page could match it cheaply. SILO's numbers are also reproducible, which Liqi's apparently are not.
5. **Restatement pairs.** Liqi's before/after informe store (28,365 pairs) is a customer-visible feature SILO lacks: SILO's `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` overwrites. A narrow append-only `*_history` table for the fields that matter (PL, inadimplência, subordinação) would let SILO answer "what did the fund first declare?"
6. **The "reader ≠ approver" rule for any LLM-assisted step SILO ever adds**, for example parsing regulamentos: model proposes, deterministic code verifies, reject rather than repair.

**Avoid:**

1. **Unversioned in-place "learning".** Anything that changes what SILO computes must be a commit (field map, migration, analytical SQL), never runtime state. SILO's advantage over Tomé is that every number is a deterministic function of (source file, git SHA). Keep it that way and **say so**. Stamping the ingest commit SHA and parser version into `cvm_ingest_log`, if not already there, would make that claim checkable per slice.
2. **Metric sprawl without a registry.** Don't publish counts (tables, pipelines, tests) that aren't generated from the system itself. Liqi's inconsistent numbers erode the trust its engineering earns. Generate SILO's from `api.catalog()`, pytest and the workflows.
3. **Agent-count bragging and autonomy claims.** For S1/S2 buyers, "one founder, deterministic pipeline, every row reproducible" is a stronger regulated-buyer story than "N autonomous agents". Liqi's own CEO ends his episode on a dependency risk ("cadê o cérebro?").
4. **Chasing unstructured-document coverage at Liqi's velocity.** Liqi's moat is 2.3M+ PDF pages read by LLMs, with 1-in-9 fabricated citations caught and 12% sampled substantive errors. Matching that means taking on the same error class. SILO should stay the **structured, reproducible system of record** and, if anything, become the ground truth that others' LLM extractions are checked against: CVM informe fields versus Tomé-style readings.
5. **High-autonomy code generation without the gate.** At ~125 commits/day, the only thing between the codebase and a silent regression is the test and verifier suite. SILO already has the gate (pre-push tests, `verify_pipeline.py`, the PostToolUse hook). The lesson is to never let session velocity bypass it, not to go faster.
